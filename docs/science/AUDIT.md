# 🔬 Scientific Audit & Methodological Provenance (`AUDIT.md`)

**Status:** Canonical Non-Parametric Trend Engine (`v1.0.0`, verified across 40 automated test suites in `tests/`).  
**Primary Domain Contexts:** Brazilian Municipal Climate Forensics (starting in **Vilhena, Rondônia — Cerrado–Amazon Ecotone**), Amazon Basin Hydro-Climatology, and Global Earth System Trend Detection (`1981–2025`).  
**Reference Implementation:** [`src/lib/stats/`](../../src/lib/stats/) & [`src/lib/nasa/`](../../src/lib/nasa/).

> **Observational Statistical Audit — Without Single-Cause Physical Attribution:**  
> All verdicts generated by **GeoTrend** quantify the **monotonic direction, magnitude, and statistical significance** of historical climate and hydrological time series (`1981–2025`) derived from NASA POWER / MERRA-2 gridded reanalysis (`0.5° × 0.625°`). A statistically significant Mann-Kendall test ($p < 0.01$ or $p < 0.05$) proves that an observed shift is unlikely to arise from random interannual noise; it **does not** by itself isolate a single physical cause (e.g., global radiative forcing vs. regional deforestation vs. multi-decadal ocean oscillations such as ENSO/AMO) and **does not** replace local meteorological station calibration or field agronomic trials.

## 1. Methodological Findings, Guardrails & Corrections

Following open-science auditing best practices, the table below documents the statistical failure modes common in amateur climate dashboards and the exact engineering guardrails implemented in `src/lib/stats/` and `src/lib/nasa/`:

| Potential Pitfall / Naive Approach | Implemented Guardrail in **GeoTrend** | Operational Scope & Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) distorted by extreme El Niño / La Niña years** | Primary rate-of-change estimation uses the non-parametric **Theil-Sen median pairwise slope** ([`sensSlope.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/sensSlope.ts)), which has a breakdown point of ~29.3% against extreme outliers. OLS slope and $R^2$ ([`regression.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/regression.ts)) are reported strictly as secondary diagnostic references. | Non-parametric median slope resists extreme single-year anomalies (e.g., 1983, 1998, 2015–2016, 2023–2024 ENSO events), but assumes an approximately monotonic shift over the selected window. |
| **Point slope reported without uncertainty bounds** | Analytical **95% confidence intervals** (`[ciLowerPerDecade, ciUpperPerDecade]`) are computed over the ranked $N = n(n-1)/2$ pairwise slopes using the Hollander-Wolfe / Gilbert normal-approximation rank indices ($C_\alpha = z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\text{Var}(S)}$). | Confidence bounds reflect sampling dispersion across the 45-year window; when confidence intervals cross zero, the trend is automatically classified as `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. |
| **Serial correlation (Lag-1 Autocorrelation) inflating Mann-Kendall Type I error** | Every time series is audited for **Lag-1 serial autocorrelation ($r_1$)** ([`regression.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/regression.ts)). Whenever $\|r_1\| > 0.30$, `warnings.highAutocorrelation = true` is triggered in the dossier metadata. | Positive persistence ($r_1 > 0.30$, common in multi-year warming and soil moisture series) increases the probability of large $\|Z\|$ values; the warning explicitly alerts investigators to inspect the 95% Sen's Slope CI alongside the raw $p$-value. |
| **Short time windows producing unstable trend claims** | Investigations with $n < 15$ valid years automatically set `warnings.shortRecord = true`, and queries with $n < 10$ years are rejected by the validation layer ([`validation.ts`](../../src/lib/nasa/validation.ts)). | Multi-decadal climate normals (WMO standard $\ge 30$ years; default NASA POWER window `1981–2025`, $n = 45$) are required to separate decadal oscillations from structural trends. |
| **Multiple hypothesis testing across variables/seasons (*p*-hacking)** | We implement the **Benjamini-Hochberg (1995) step-up False Discovery Rate (FDR)** procedure ([`benjaminiHochberg.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/benjaminiHochberg.ts)) to compute adjusted $q$-values when screening multiple variable-season combinations simultaneously. | Prevents false-positive inflation when scanning all 4 variables (`T2M`, `T2M_MIN`, `PRECTOTCORR`, `GWETROOT`) across 3 seasonal windows (`Annual`, `Oct–Mar`, `Apr–Sep`). |
| **NASA POWER `-999` fill values corrupting seasonal aggregates** | [`aggregate.ts`](../../src/lib/nasa/aggregate.ts) strips all sentinel values (`-999`, `-999.0`, `NaN`, non-finite numbers) before aggregation and enforces a **$\ge 80\%$ valid-month completeness threshold** per year/season (minimum 10 of 12 months for `Annual`; 5 of 6 months for `Oct–Mar` and `Apr–Sep`). | Incomplete tail years (e.g., partial current calendar year) are automatically excluded rather than downward-biasing cumulative rainfall (`PRECTOTCORR`). |
| **Southern Hemisphere hydrological year split across calendar years** | `Oct–Mar` wet-season aggregation pairs **October–December of year $Y-1$ with January–March of year $Y$** ([`aggregate.ts`](../../src/lib/nasa/aggregate.ts)), preserving the physical Amazonian/Cerrado agricultural rainy season. | Consequently, a `1981–2025` query yields $n = 45$ complete years for `Annual` and `Apr–Sep`, and $n = 44$ complete hydrological seasons (`1982–2025`) for `Oct–Mar`. |
| **Dimensionless soil wetness (`0–1`) misread by non-specialists** | `GWETROOT` is converted from dimensionless fraction (`0–1`) to percentage points (`0–100%`) at ingestion, and `PRECTOTCORR` daily rates ($\text{mm/day}$) are multiplied by exact calendar month length (including leap-year Februaries) before summing into seasonal totals ($\text{mm}$). | Units in exported CSVs and citations match physical agro-hydrological conventions ($\text{°C}$, $\text{mm}$, $\text{\%}$). |

## 2. Mathematical Formula & Provenance Matrix

All statistical computations are deterministic, zero-dependency TypeScript modules located in [`src/lib/stats/`](../../src/lib/stats/) and verified via Test-Driven Development (TDD) against analytical reference datasets.

| Method / Metric | Mathematical Formulation Implemented | Module & Function | Primary Scientific Reference (DOI) | Interpretation & Scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mann-Kendall Statistic ($S$)** | $$S = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \sum_{j=i+1}^{n} \operatorname{sgn}(x_j - x_i)$$ | [`mannKendall.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/mannKendall.ts) `mannKendallTest()` | [Mann (1945)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1907187); [Kendall (1975)](https://archive.org/details/rankcorrelationm0000kend) | Non-parametric rank concordance sum; requires no Gaussian distribution assumption. |
| **Tie-Corrected Variance $\operatorname{Var}(S)$** | $$\operatorname{Var}(S) = \frac{n(n-1)(2n+5) - \sum_{p=1}^{g} t_p(t_p-1)(2t_p+5)}{18}$$ | [`mannKendall.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/mannKendall.ts) `mannKendallTest()` | [Kendall (1975)](https://archive.org/details/rankcorrelationm0000kend); [Gilbert (1987)](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/7037501) | Accounts for tied seasonal observations ($t_p$ ties in group $p$). |
| **Continuity-Corrected $Z$-Score & Two-Sided $p$-Value** | $$Z = \begin{cases} \frac{S - 1}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(S)}} & S > 0 \\ 0 & S = 0 \\ \frac{S + 1}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(S)}} & S < 0 \end{cases}, \quad p = 2\bigl(1 - \Phi(\|Z\|)\bigr)$$ | [`mannKendall.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/mannKendall.ts) `mannKendallTest()` | [Gilbert (1987)](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/7037501) | Standard normal CDF $\Phi$ evaluated via Abramowitz & Stegun (1964) formula 7.1.26 (max error $1.5 \times 10^{-7}$). |
| **Theil-Sen Robust Slope ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{Sen}}$)** | $$\hat{\beta}_{\text{Sen}} = \operatorname{median}_{1 \le i < j \le n} \left( \frac{x_j - x_i}{t_j - t_i} \right), \quad \hat{\beta}_{10\text{yr}} = 10\,\hat{\beta}_{\text{Sen}}$$ | [`sensSlope.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/sensSlope.ts) `sensSlope()` | [Theil (1950)](https://dwc.knaw.nl/DL/publications/PU00018855.pdf); [Sen (1968)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1968.10480934) | Robust annual and decadal rate of change; anchored at median time $\tilde{t}$ and median value $\tilde{x}$. |
| **95% Confidence Interval on Sen's Slope** | $$M_1 = \frac{N - z_{0.975}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(S)}}{2}, \quad M_2 = \frac{N + z_{0.975}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(S)}}{2}$$ | [`sensSlope.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/sensSlope.ts) `sensSlope()` | [Hollander & Wolfe (1973)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/9781119196037); [Gilbert (1987)](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/7037501) | Lower and upper 95% slope bounds interpolated from sorted $N = n(n-1)/2$ pairwise slopes. |
| **Parametric OLS Slope & $R^2$** | $$\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} = \frac{\sum (t_i - \bar{t})(x_i - \bar{x})}{\sum (t_i - \bar{t})^2}, \quad R^2 = r_{tx}^2$$ | [`regression.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/regression.ts) `linearRegression()` | Standard Gauss-Markov OLS | Reported for side-by-side comparison with non-parametric Sen's Slope. |
| **Lag-1 Serial Autocorrelation ($r_1$)** | $$r_1 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_i - \bar{x})(x_{i+1} - \bar{x})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \bar{x})^2}$$ | [`regression.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/regression.ts) `lag1Autocorrelation()` | [Box & Jenkins (1976)](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Time+Series+Analysis%3A+Forecasting+and+Control-p-9781118675021); [Hamed & Rao (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-1694(97)00125-X) | Flags serial persistence ($\|r_1\| > 0.30$) that can inflate Mann-Kendall $Z$-scores. |
| **Benjamini-Hochberg FDR ($q$-Value)** | $$q_{(k)} = \min_{m \ge k}\left(1,\; \frac{M}{m}\,p_{(m)}\right)$$ | [`benjaminiHochberg.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/benjaminiHochberg.ts) `benjaminiHochberg()` | [Benjamini & Hochberg (1995)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x) | Controls False Discovery Rate when screening $M$ hypotheses simultaneously. |
| **Standardized Anomaly ($z_i$)** | $$z_i = \frac{x_i - \mu}{\sigma}, \quad \text{Extreme Anomaly} \iff \|z_i\| > 1.5$$ | [`analyzeTrend.ts`](../../src/lib/stats/analyzeTrend.ts) `analyzeTrend()` | WMO Climatological Anomaly Convention | Identifies extreme anomaly years (`isExtremeAnomaly`) and normalizes *Show Your Stripes* color bands. |

## 5. Field Cross-Validation & Regional Integration Protocol (*Missão Amazônia*)

To transition from satellite/reanalysis trend detection (`0.5° × 0.625°`) to local territorial action in **Rondônia and the Brazilian Amazon**, investigators, agronomists, and educators should apply the following 3-step cross-validation protocol:

1. **Meteorological & Hydrological Ground-Truthing:**
   - Cross-check NASA POWER grid-cell trends against local rain-gauge and river-stage records maintained by **INMET** (Vilhena Automatic Station `A938`), **ANA HidroWeb** (Ji-Paraná / Machado and Guaporé basins), and **CPRM / SGB**.
2. **Land-Use & Vegetation Context (*MapBiomas* & NASA MODIS):**
   - Inspect the integrated `<SatelliteTimelapse />` (`MODIS True Color` & `NDVI`, `2000–2025`) alongside **MapBiomas Collection 9** municipal land-cover transitions to evaluate whether local surface temperature (`T2M_MIN`) or soil wetness (`GWETROOT`) shifts co-occur with canopy conversion or pasture degradation.
3. **Agronomic & Educational Application (UN SDGs 6, 13, 15):**
   - Combine the exported **CSV + Academic Citation** from `<ShareSheet />` with **EMBRAPA ZARC** (*Zoneamento Agrícola de Risco Climático*) tables for second-crop (*safrinha*) planting windows in the Southern Cone of Rondônia, and use the visual *Show Your Stripes* Evidence Cards in high-school and university environmental science laboratories.
